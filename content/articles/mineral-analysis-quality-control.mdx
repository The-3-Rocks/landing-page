### Laboratory Testing Methods

#### X-Ray Fluorescence (XRF) Analysis
**Primary Use**: Rapid elemental composition analysis

- **How It Works**: X-rays excite atoms in the mineral sample, causing them to emit characteristic fluorescent radiation
- **Advantages**: Non-destructive, fast results (2-5 minutes), minimal sample preparation
- **Applications**: Lead (86% purity), copper (8-21%), zinc (+37%), iron (≥56%)
- **Accuracy**: ±0.5% for major elements
- **Sample Size**: 1-10 grams

**Moroccan Standards**: Our facilities use Bruker and Thermo Fisher XRF equipment calibrated to ASTM E1621 standards.

#### Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (ICP-MS)
**Primary Use**: Trace element and high-precision analysis

-**Precision**: Parts per billion (ppb) detection
- **Process**:
  1. Sample dissolution in acid
  2. Nebulization into argon plasma (7,000-10,000°C)
  3. Mass spectrometer detection
- **Applications**: Cobalt (42%+), antimony (30%+), trace contaminants
- **Analysis Time**: 15-30 minutes per sample

**Quality Assurance**: ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratories in Morocco

#### Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS)
**Primary Use**: Metal concentration measurement

- **Technique**: Measures light absorption by atomized sample
- **Sensitivity**: Parts per million (ppm) to parts per billion (ppb)
- **Best For**: Copper, zinc, lead, iron content verification
- **Sample Preparation**: Acid digestion required

### Physical Property Testing

#### Density Measurement (Barite Specification)
- **Target**: 4.18-4.28 specific gravity for barite
- **Method**: Pycnometry or Archimedes principle
- **Importance**: Critical for drilling fluid applications
- **Standard**: API Specification 13A

#### Particle Size Distribution
- **Methods**:
  - **Sieve Analysis**: For larger particles (ore)
  - **Laser Diffraction**: For powders and concentrates
  - **Sedimentation**: For very fine materials
- **Application**: Lead powder (76% purity), barite powder

#### Moisture Content Analysis
- **Standard**: ASTM D2216
- **Target**: \u003c2% for concentrates, variable for ore
- **Impact**: Affects shipping weight and processing

### Mineralogical Analysis

#### X-Ray Diffraction (XRD)
**Purpose**: Crystal structure and mineral phase identification

- **Technology**: Analyzes diffraction patterns from crystal lattices
- **Information Provided**:
  - Mineral composition (galena, chalcopyrite, sphalerite, etc.)
  - Crystal structure
  - Quantitative phase analysis
- **Sample Requirements**: Powdered sample, 1-5 grams

#### Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM-EDS)
**Purpose**: Microstructural analysis and elemental mapping

- **Magnification**: Up to 100,000x
- **Capabilities**:
  - Surface morphology
  - Particle size and shape
  - Energy Dispersive Spectroscopy (EDS) for elemental composition
  - Contamination identification

###Quality Control Process

#### 1. Sampling Protocol
- **Representative Sampling**: Following ISO 12744 guidelines
- **Sample Reduction**: Riffle splitters, cone-and-quarter method
- **Duplicate Samples**: 10% of all samples for quality control
- **Retention Samples**: Archived for 2 years

#### 2. Calibration & Standards
- **Certified Reference Materials (CRMs)**: NIST, CANMET, USGS standards
- **Internal Standards**: Matrix-matched standards for each mineral type
- **Calibration Frequency**: Daily for XRF, weekly for ICP-MS
- **Quality Control Charts**: Shewhart control charts for monitoring

#### 3. Inter-laboratory Testing
- **Round Robin Tests**: Quarterly participation with international labs
- **Blind Samples**: Monthly verification with third-party testing
- **Accreditation**: ISO/IEC 17025 for testing competence
